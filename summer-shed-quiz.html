Summer shed and cat allergy quiz

Understand and fix your flare-up with a 1 minute quiz

Is your cat making you stuffy?

Lots of cat owners feel worse in late spring and early summer and never quite know why. Often it isn’t only pollen: cats shed their winter coat then, which can flood your home with extra Fel d 1. This one-minute quiz helps you see if that pattern affects you.

Did you know?

Millions blame pollen and the weather first—while the same weeks often bring a jump in indoor Fel d 1 from a cat shedding its winter coat.

  • Late spring · early summer
  • Warmer days, heavier shed
  • More Fel d 1 in the room

This isn’t random—it’s a season

When days warm up, many cats shed the thick winter coat they grew when it was cold. Loose hair and skin flakes carry Fel d 1 into the air and onto couches, beds, and clothes. If your nose reads that jump as “mystery allergies,” you are in good company—but the calendar and the vacuum canister often tell a clearer story than pollen alone.

  • Same cat, more allergen in the environment during heavy shed windows—not a new “allergy to your cat,” just more of the protein around you.
  • Soft surfaces hold the payload where cats nap; that is where a neutralizing spray can complement brushing, baths (if your cat tolerates them), and washing bedding.
